## Insights

- The 560x420 profile improved real-test pose quality strongly versus the July
  10 run: best mixed ADD(-S)<0.1d rose from `0.109` to `0.240`, median
  normalized error dropped from `0.541d` to `0.248d`, and median rotation error
  dropped from `26.5 deg` to `8.1 deg`.
- Reference geometry also improved: best normalized reference Chamfer improved
  from `0.038d` to `0.015d`. This supports the idea that preserving more image
  information at 4:3 resolution helps the onboarding/reference constellation.
- The held-out PBR split is much easier than the real test split. At `N=5,K=1`,
  held-out PBR reaches `0.411` ADD(-S)<0.1d versus `0.240` on real test. This
  keeps the synthetic-to-real domain gap as the main remaining bottleneck.
- More query context on held-out PBR helps a lot. The PBR subsets go from
  roughly `0.41` at `K=1` to `0.68` at `K=5..10`, with median errors around
  `0.064d..0.069d` and rotation medians near `1.3..1.4 deg`.
- More reference views alone did not solve the real-test gap. `N=16,K=1` is
  very close to `N=5,K=1` on real test, while it improves reference Chamfer.
  This points again to query association/domain transfer rather than reference
  reconstruction alone.
- Object behavior is highly uneven. Objects 5, 8, and 11 are much stronger on
  real images; objects 6 and 10 remain difficult despite better median errors.
  Object 10 becomes excellent on held-out PBR, which suggests its real-test
  failure is not simply geometric impossibility.
- The new bucketed metrics make the failure mode much clearer: big/visible
  objects are consistently easier than small/occluded objects. On real test,
  small occluded recall is `0.000`, while big visible recall is about `0.32`.
- Easy-vs-hard buckets separate cleanly, but both still improve under the PBR
  multi-query setting. This means the model can exploit geometry/context when
  the domain is controlled; the largest gap is real-image transfer.
